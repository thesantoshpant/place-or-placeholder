Paper 1 — Place or Placeholder? Evaluation Plan
Nov 15, 2026 · @Adithya
Summary
Paper 1 tests whether satellite embeddings genuinely improve dengue forecasts, or merely act as a
district ID. Target: a preprint and journal submission by the end of April 2027, on about 5–20 GPU-hours.
Working title: Place or placeholder? When satellite embeddings genuinely improve dengue forecasts.
Why now: two papers from the last six weeks report that Google's place embeddings improve dengue
forecasts, in Brazil (Brittain et al.) and Mexico (Muslim et al.). Both used one embedding snapshot from
October 2023 for forecasts back to 2020. Neither compared the embeddings with a plain district ID, and
neither tested on districts the model never saw. The AlphaEarth health paper (Nazir et al.) has the same
gaps.
Contribution: the first test of satellite embeddings for dengue forecasting that does all three:
1. aligns every embedding in time, so only images from before the forecast date are used
2. compares against location-only controls (a plain district ID or coordinates)
3. tests on districts, states and a country the model never trained on
It covers four countries, using English-language data from OpenDengue.
Either result is publishable. If the gains vanish under these controls, that corrects a fast-growing
literature. If they survive, we know where imagery helps, which designs Paper 2's model.
Target venues: a medRxiv preprint, then Epidemics or PLOS Computational Biology. A short version can
go to a spring 2027 ML workshop.
Research questions and hypotheses
The paper reports two numbers for every split. Embedding gain is how much the forecast improves
when satellite embeddings are added. ID share is how much of that gain a location-only control
reproduces.
\text{Embedding gain}_
s = \mathrm{WIS}_
s(L3) - \mathrm{WIS}_
s(L4)
\text{ID share}_
s = \frac{\mathrm{WIS}_
s(L3) - \mathrm{WIS}_
s(L3 + \text{best ID
control})}{\text{Embedding gain}_
s}
Here s is the split and WIS is the weighted interval score (lower is better). L3 and L4 are the feature levels
defined under Study design. An ID share near 1 means the embedding is acting as a district label. Near 0
means it carries information a label cannot.

Embeddings are averaged within each district, weighted by population. Unweighted means are a
sensitivity check.
Earth Engine quota: the free Community tier gives 150 compute-hours a month. Pooling ~7,000 districts
× 8 years should fit. If it does not, the Contributor tier (1,000 hours) only needs a linked billing account
and is not charged.
Study design
The experiment is a grid: 6 feature levels × 5 controls × 6 splits × 4 models, scored the same way
everywhere. Each cell is cheap to run. The value is in running all of them under one protocol.
Prediction target
•
Weekly reported dengue cases per district, modelled as log(1 + cases). Results are reported back
on the case scale.

Buffer width for S3 comes from the range of the case correlogram, estimated in week 2.
Models
1. Seasonal naive (L0 only).
2. Negative binomial regression: the standard epidemiology baseline.
3. LightGBM with quantile loss: the main model, fast enough for the whole grid.
4. TimesFM 2.5 zero-shot forecasts, with LightGBM correcting the residuals using the extra
features. This shows whether a modern forecasting foundation model changes the answer.
Metrics
•
•
•
•
Weighted interval score (WIS) from 23 quantiles, the standard used by forecasting hubs. This is
the primary metric.
Coverage of the 50% and 90% intervals.
Mean absolute error on the case scale.
Outbreak detection: an outbreak week is above that district's 2013–2019 90th percentile. Report
sensitivity at a fixed 10% false-alarm rate.
Statistics
•
•
•
Diebold–Mariano tests on WIS differences, with Harvey's small-sample correction.
Moving-block bootstrap (block of 8 weeks, resampling districts within state) for confidence
intervals on embedding gain and ID share.
Holm correction across the main comparisons. The primary comparisons are fixed before any
test-set result is seen (see the January design freeze).
Team
