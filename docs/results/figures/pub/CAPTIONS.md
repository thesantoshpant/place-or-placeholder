# Figure captions

Generated by `scripts/paper/make_pub_figures.py` from the results-kit tables (`docs/results/tables/*.csv`); cell counts, the selected control, Holm denominators and pending notes update on rerun. These captions describe exactly what is drawn. WIS = weighted interval score on log(1 + cases); lower is better. Reference WIS = WIS of the reference model L3 (N3 without case history) on the same rows.

**Fig 1. Study design and information timing.** Each comparison changes one block of inputs while the model type, scored rows and configuration stay fixed; primary scores use only forecasts whose inputs and training labels end at the origin. (A) What is compared: the ladder L0–L4 and, from the reference model L3, the 2 × 2 grid behind the three gains: the embedding gain (L3 to L4), the control gain (L3 to L3 + C*) and the excess gain (L3 + C* to L4 + C*; primary). The location control C* is chosen at the 2019 origin from C1 historical risk, C2 coordinates, C3 shuffled embedding, C4/C4s random smooth fields and C5 2023 snapshot; C6 (district identifier) is a candidate only in the analysis without case history. (B) Where models are tested, drawn on generic clusters of hexagons standing for districts (light: training; dark: test; dashed: dropped buffer): S1, new years; S2, held-out states; S3, held-out regions; S4, held-out districts; S5, held-out country. S2–S4 were run in Brazil and Colombia, S1 and S5 in all four countries; held-out districts keep their own lagged cases. (C) Information timing for one forecast. The example origin o is the end of epidemiological week 6 of year Y, so the 12-week case-history and climate windows (o − 11 to o) run from week 47 of year Y − 1 to week 6 of year Y (bold tick labels; a 52-week year is drawn). Remote sensing uses the last three complete months before the month of o; yearly inputs come from the year before the target's epidemiological year; training targets end in the last week of Y − 1. Targets in the first h weeks of Y (origin in Y − 1; hatched) are dropped from primary scoring. Horizons h are 1 to 4 weeks; primary estimates pool horizons 1 and 4.

**Fig 2. Excess and embedding gains from AlphaEarth embeddings in each country and split.** For each cell, the filled marker is the excess gain (L3 + C* to L4 + C*; primary) and the open marker, drawn just below it, is the embedding gain (L3 to L4; co-primary), both as a percentage of the reference WIS (weeks weighted equally; origin-year rows; horizons 1 and 4 pooled). Positive values mean that the embeddings improve the forecast. Lines are 95% confidence intervals from Diebold–Mariano tests with the EWC variance and the HLN correction. The shaded band is the ±1% equivalence margin and the dashed lines mark ±0.5%. The right-hand columns give each gain with its 95% CI (embedding gain in grey) and the Holm-adjusted p value of the excess gain (Holm adjustment over the 13 available cells, final family 14; bold: p < 0.05). Taiwan, held-out country (S5) is drawn on its own scale in the left panel after an axis break: excess gain −17.3% (absolute −0.0035 log-WIS) and embedding gain −21.6% (absolute −0.0044). Taiwan cells include test year 2021. Results not yet available: Brazil held-out districts (S4). S1, new years; S2, held-out states; S3, held-out regions; S4, held-out districts; S5, held-out country.

**Fig 3. Contributions of three types of target week to the excess gain.** For each cell, the excess gain of Fig 2 is split additively into contributions from weeks with cases, zero weeks listed by the source (recorded zeros; Brazil 2020–2021 only) and zero weeks filled by us, each in percentage points of the cell's reference WIS, with 95% confidence intervals (unadjusted; filled marker: interval excludes 0). The three contributions add up exactly to the total printed in the right-hand column (no total marker is drawn). Interval ends beyond the axis are cut with an arrowhead; an estimate beyond the axis is drawn as an arrow at the panel edge with its value printed beside it (Taiwan, held-out country, zero-filled weeks). The strata are defined by the outcome and are descriptive. Taiwan cells include test year 2021. S1, new years; S2, held-out states; S3, held-out regions; S4, held-out districts; S5, held-out country.

**Fig 4. Forecasting without case history (Brazil, new years; exploratory).** Models use season, climate, remote sensing and population only (reference model N3), plus a location input. (A) Mean WIS over rows for each model, horizons 1 and 4 pooled (seed 0; the same rows in every model). C* is C2 (coordinates), chosen at the 2019 origin; C1 (pre-2018 case average, static history) is a separate rung, not a candidate. (B) Control gain (N3 to N3 + C*), embedding gain (N3 to N4) and excess gain (N3 + C* to N4 + C*) as a percentage of the reference WIS with weeks weighted equally, so panel B percentages use a slightly different denominator from the row means in panel A; 95% confidence intervals (filled marker: interval excludes 0). The shaded band is ±1% and the dashed lines ±0.5%. Adding coordinates alone improves forecasts (Brazil S1). The embeddings add no detectable skill once coordinates are in the model. Only Brazil, new years (S1) is drawn; Brazil, held-out states (S2), is added on rerun once its results are released.

**Fig S1. Model ladder in the new-years split (S1), by country.** Reduction in mean WIS over rows relative to L1 (case history only; open marker at 0), horizons 1 and 4 pooled, for L0 to L4 and for L3 and L4 with the selected location control (C* = C1, historical risk, in every country). Values beyond a panel are drawn as an arrow with their value (the seasonal naive L0 everywhere). Panels have their own scales. The Taiwan panel uses 113,430 rows without test year 2021 (the L0–L2 runs have no 2021 forecasts), not the 141,645 rows of the primary cell, and its L2 model was tuned separately, so its L2 point is not comparable with the other rungs.

**Fig S2. Excess gain by test year.** Each point is one cell and test year, as a percentage of that year's reference WIS (weeks weighted equally), horizons 1 and 4 pooled, with 95% confidence intervals (unadjusted; filled marker: interval excludes 0); marker shape gives the split. Shaded years are the COVID-19 pandemic years 2020–2021 (in the Taiwan panel only 2020 is shaded, and 2021 is labelled "no local cases"). Intervals running past a panel are cut with an arrowhead. Taiwan includes 2021, when there were no local cases, so its reference WIS is tiny and its percentages are very large (absolute values in Table S30). Panels have their own scales.

**Fig S3. Embedding similarity and the excess gain when a whole country is held out (S5).** The districts of the held-out country are split into fifths by the maximum cosine similarity between their renormalised AlphaEarth embedding (year y − 1) and those of the training districts. Points show each fifth's mean similarity and its excess gain as a percentage of the cell's overall reference WIS, with 95% confidence intervals; ρ is the district-level Spearman correlation between similarity and excess gain (naive p values in Table S39). Panels have their own vertical scales. Exploratory.
