# Figure captions (suggested)

Generated by `scripts/paper/make_pub_figures.py` from the results-kit tables; cell counts, the selected control and pending notes update on rerun. WIS = weighted interval score on log(1 + cases); lower is better.

**Fig 1. Study design and information timing.** Each comparison changes one block of inputs while the model, rows and configuration stay fixed; primary scores use only forecasts whose inputs and training labels end at the origin. (A) The feature ladder L0–L4 and the four models behind the three gains: the embedding gain (L3 to L4), the identity gain (L3 to L3 + C*) and the primary excess gain (L3 + C* to L4 + C*); the location control C* is chosen at the 2019 origin. (B) The five evaluation splits, drawn on generic clusters of hexagons standing for districts (light: training, dark: test, dashed: dropped buffer). (C) What one forecast at origin o may use: case and climate inputs up to week o, remote sensing from the last three complete months, yearly inputs from the year before the target's epidemiological year, and training targets up to the end of the previous year. Targets in the first h weeks of a year (origin in the previous year) are dropped from primary scoring.

**Fig 2. Excess gain from AlphaEarth embeddings in each country and evaluation split.** Points show the reduction in mean WIS when the embeddings are added to a model that already has the validation-selected location control (L3 + C* to L4 + C*), as a percentage of WIS(L3); positive values mean the embeddings improved forecasts. Horizons 1 and 4 weeks pooled; lines are 95% confidence intervals from Diebold–Mariano tests with an EWC variance estimator and the HLN correction. Filled points: Holm-adjusted p < 0.05; Holm's correction is over the 13 available cells; the final family has 14. The grey band marks the ±1% equivalence margin. Taiwan S5 is drawn on its own scale at left (axis break). Cells not yet available: Brazil S4. S1 new years; S2 held-out states; S3 held-out buffered blocks; S4 held-out random districts; S5 held-out country.

**Fig 3. Where the excess gain comes from.** The excess gain of Fig 2 split additively into contributions from three kinds of target weeks: weeks with cases, zeros listed by the source (recorded zeros, Brazil 2020–2021 only) and weeks we filled with zero; the three contributions sum to the total at right. Values are percentage points of WIS(L3), horizons 1 and 4 pooled, with 95% confidence intervals (filled: interval excludes 0). Strata depend on the outcome and are descriptive. Off-scale values are printed beside an arrow at the panel edge.

**Fig 4. Forecasting without case history (Brazil, exploratory arm).** Models use climate, remote sensing and population only (N3), plus a location input. (A) Mean WIS for each model, horizons 1 and 4 pooled; C* is C2 (coordinates), chosen at the 2019 origin, and C1 (the district's pre-2018 case average) is a separate static-history rung. (B) Gains as a percentage of the base model's WIS, with 95% confidence intervals (filled: interval excludes 0); the grey band marks ±1%. Adding coordinates alone improves forecasts (Brazil S1). The embeddings add no detectable skill once coordinates are in the model. Brazil S2 (the arm's headline split) is not yet in the kit tables; the figure adds it automatically when it appears.

**Fig S1. Embedding similarity and the excess gain when a whole country is held out (S5).** Districts of the held-out country are split into fifths by the cosine similarity between their AlphaEarth embedding and that of the most similar training district. Points show each fifth's excess gain as a percentage of the country's overall WIS(L3), with 95% confidence intervals; ρ is the district-level Spearman correlation between similarity and gain. Vertical scales differ between panels.

**Fig S2. Excess gain by test year.** Each point is one country × split × test year, as a percentage of that year's WIS(L3), horizons 1 and 4 pooled, with 95% confidence intervals (filled: interval excludes 0); marker shape gives the split. Shaded years are COVID-era reporting. Intervals running past the panel are cut with an arrowhead; in Taiwan 2021 there were no local cases, so WIS(L3) is tiny and percentages are very large. Vertical scales differ between panels.

**Fig S3. The feature ladder in the new-years split (S1).** Reduction in mean WIS relative to L1 (case history only), horizons 1 and 4 pooled, for each level and for L3/L4 with the validation-selected location control. Points beyond the panel are drawn as arrows with their value. Taiwan: the ladder table covers 113,430 rows, not the 141,645 of the primary cell (Fig 2).
