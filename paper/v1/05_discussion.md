Because case history already identifies every district, no control in the main analysis can test whether embeddings act as place information or as a placeholder; with no detectable gain, the question did not arise. Held-out districts kept their own case history, so the spatial splits removed only the chance to memorise which embedding belongs to which district. The identity question rests on the analysis without case history (Section 4.3).

The null result may not be specific to embeddings. At one and four weeks, recent case counts carried nearly all the skill: the whole non-case stack was worth about 1.1% in Brazil and 0.45% in Colombia. <!-- src: T2_ladder.csv BR_S1, CO_S1 L1 vs L3 row means --> This is the key limit on scope. The task may be insensitive to any static description of place, so our results say whether embeddings add skill to a realistic short-term model, not whether they contain information about dengue risk. ### 4.2 Why the embeddings hurt where they do

Annual embeddings differ between districts but not within a season, so trees use them mainly to shift a district's forecast level; they raised the upper quantiles on zero weeks in every cell (Section 3.5). <!-- src: S6_q90_mechanism.csv dq90_log (13/13 positive; CI excludes 0 in 12) --> The losses on zero weeks and gains on case weeks follow from that shift and are not, on their own, evidence of worse or better forecasting [@lerch2017dilemma]. Case-week intervals covered only 61% to 75% at the 80% level, so a raised upper tail could partly correct under-dispersion [pending]. <!-- src: T2_ladder.csv cov_80_nonzero of L4+C* --> <!-- PENDING: R3-A6 (b) coverage and width change on case and zero weeks --> Colombia's losses arose mainly in zero-filled weeks, which were validated only in aggregate; if some of those zeros hide unreported cases, part of the "loss" penalises a correct upward shift. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv CO_S1–CO_S4 zero_filled contrib_pct (−0.59 to −0.76) vs total_pct (−0.39 to −0.65) --> <!-- PENDING: R3-A6 (a) CO district-years with ≥1 case -->

Part of the loss is generic: a shuffled embedding of the same size cost one-third to four-fifths as much as the real embeddings (Section 3.6). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv C3 G_id_pct / G_emb_pct: colombia −0.12/−0.36 = 0.33, peru −0.76/−0.94 = 0.81, taiwan −0.59/−0.94 = 0.63 (Taiwan without 2021) --> Sixty-four extra columns that are nearly constant within a district give the trees more ways to split districts into groups, whatever the columns contain. Year-to-year noise may add to this: a district's embedding moved about as much between years as it differed from its neighbours (Section 3.7), so a model trained on earlier years meets slightly shifted values in the test year. <!-- PENDING: R3-A7 (supervisor 2026-11-19: within-district year-to-year cosine ~0.98 vs neighbours ~0.97) --> Lower-dimensional and static forms of the embeddings test both explanations (Section 3.9) [pending]. <!-- PENDING: R3-C EXP-A V1, V2 --> AlphaEarth was also trained to reconstruct climate reanalysis and elevation [@brown2025alphaearth], so its content may partly duplicate our inputs. <!-- src: Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 (ERA5-Land and GLO-30 DEM as training targets), read 2026-11-18 -->

Capacity matters too: in Brazil the gap between the shared configuration and per-model tuning was about eight times the embedding gain (Supplementary Methods, section C), so comparisons that tune each input set separately partly measure the tuning. <!-- src: S1 section C (T2_ladder_long.csv: 0.00327/0.00043 = 7.7 pooled h{1,4}) --> The shared configuration was tuned on the case-history model, so it may not suit 150 inputs; equal-budget tuning in Colombia gave [pending]. <!-- PENDING: R3-C EXP-B S4 --> The losses do not depend on the larger trees: five of the six cells with a loss used Colombia's 31-leaf configuration, and the sixth, Taiwan held out, used Brazil's 127-leaf configuration. <!-- src: docs/methods/split_pipeline.md "Params" (Colombia 31/100 for CO_S1–CO_S4; Colombia's configuration for BR_S5; Brazil's 127/100 for TW_S5) -->

Taiwan held out is a stress test: 98% of rows were zero-filled, its embeddings lay far from the training districts', and the embeddings raised its zero-row 0.9 quantile by 0.099 log units. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv TW_S5 zero_filled row_share 0.98; S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 cos_max_mean 0.70–0.82; S6_q90_mechanism.csv TW_S5 dq90_log --> The loss did not grow with distance in embedding space: every township lost (Section 3.7). <!-- src: S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 G_excess_share_units_neg = 1 in every quintile (285/285, with 2021); S7_embedding_distance.csv TW_S5 G_excess cos_max spearman_rho --> A uniform shift fits embeddings that separate countries and so carry each country's case definition and reporting level: the training countries count probable or total cases, while Taiwan counts only locally acquired confirmed cases. ### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

Our results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used a different embedding, monthly targets and a residual adjustment to a strong baseline [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary], but they suggest that small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control before they are read as place information. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we found no embedding advantage beyond location, here across four countries, weekly probabilistic forecasts and held-out places. In the Brazilian new-years control panel, the point estimate fell from +0.29% without a control to +0.02% with coordinates, though neither differed from zero. <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29 (seed 0), C2 G_excess_pct 0.02 (CI −0.25 to 0.29) --> They also fit evidence that location proxies serve interpolation better than extrapolation [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies] and that embedding-location confounding depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite]. AlphaEarth gains reported for annual malaria and survey-based outcomes [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories, closer to our analysis without case history.

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

Strengths include four countries, fixed rows and capacity across the ladder, several location controls, equivalence testing, independent checks of filled zeros and a dated deviation log. The null result held under weighting, recalibration, the case scale and all rows, apart from two nominal, unadjusted gains (Colombia held out with case weights, +0.49%; Peru held out on all rows, +1.01%). <!-- src: S4_weighted.csv (CO_S5 pre_cases_plus1 +0.49, p 0.026); S5_recalibration.csv; T1_primary_family.csv case_pct, case_p, unfiltered_pct, unfiltered_p (PE_S5 +1.01, p 0.046) -->

The main external-validity gap is that we did not test PDFM, the embedding used in both earlier dengue studies; it requires approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm], and our results apply to AlphaEarth only. We tested one model class; other learners, a residual design like those studies' and learned location encoders [@vivanco2023geoclip; @klemmer2025satclip] may behave differently.

Final, revised counts make absolute skill optimistic. Because AlphaEarth starts in 2017, the 2020 models learned from two years only, and the protocol's longer training window was not run. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 35 --> In Brazil the excess gain rose with each added training year, from −0.22% in 2020 to +0.88% in 2024 (Section 3.5); whether gains emerge with longer histories is untested. <!-- src: S8_per_year.csv BR_S1 G_excess pct_of_ref --> <!-- PENDING: R3-A3 trend test --> The AlphaEarth model was trained on imagery sampled from 2017 to the present, overlapping our 2020–2024 test years, and one model produced every annual layer after training [@brown2025alphaearth]; this can only favour the embeddings and so strengthens the null. <!-- src: R8_literature.md section 4 (Brown et al. §S15: training sequences "covering the period 2017 to present"; §S22.2: annual layers generated after training); R10_fact_check.md row 2 --> Espírito Santo's false zeros remain in the training targets. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34 -->

### 4.6 Implications and future work

We found no evidence of benefit, but small gains are not excluded: the upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil (new years) and +2.05% with Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> We make no recommendation about operational use. Follow-up work will test PDFM for Brazil, a second model class and a residual design on zero-shot TimesFM, embeddings centred within each country, tuning of the analysis without case history on its own reference model, equal-budget tuning and more seeds in Brazil, longer horizons and monthly targets, and data with realistic reporting delays.

### 4.7 Conclusions

Across four countries, with case history in the model, AlphaEarth embeddings added no detectable skill to weekly district dengue forecasts at one and four weeks, so whether they act as place information or as an identifier did not arise. Effects larger than about the combined benefit of climate, remote sensing and population in Brazil were excluded in 8 of 13 cells with the primary variance estimator, fewer with a cautious one [pending]. <!-- PENDING: BR S4, final Holm m=14, R3-A1, R3-A4 --> <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/13) --> One small loss was robust to every check, and embeddings may worsen transfer to a country with different surveillance. In Brazil without case history, coordinates improved forecasts while the embeddings added no detectable skill beyond them, which is consistent with, but does not establish, a location proxy. Gains from foundation-model features should be tested against location-only controls, at fixed capacity, on held-out places and against a stated equivalence margin before they are attributed to information about place.
