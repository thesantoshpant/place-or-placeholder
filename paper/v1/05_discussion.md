### 4.1 Principal findings

With surveillance history in the model, we found no evidence that AlphaEarth embeddings improve weekly district dengue forecasts. No cell showed a gain when only the years were new, in unseen places, or beyond location controls, which themselves added no detectable skill; the embeddings harmed transfer to Taiwan. Losses arose mainly in weeks without cases, and gains, where present, in weeks with cases. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, Holm_p_available, G_id_pct, G_id_lo95_pct; T5_strata.csv G_excess contrib_pct by stratum --> No excess gain survived correction for multiple testing, and differences larger than ±1% were excluded in 8 of 13 cells [pending]. <!-- PENDING: BR S4 and final Holm m=14: recheck "8 of 13" and "six cells" in this paragraph --> <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/13) --> Of the six losses, five were small and one was large relative to a low baseline (Taiwan held out; absolute difference 0.0035), <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, G_excess, Holm_p_available, loss_or_gain --> and two (Brazil held out; Colombia, held-out states) remained significant after correction under both more cautious checks. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv B4_Holm_p, p_iaaft_Holm (BR_S5 0.027/0.0065; CO_S2 0.028/0.0065) --> Because case history already identifies the district, these results cannot settle whether the embeddings act as place information or as a placeholder.

The null result is not specific to embeddings. At one- and four-week horizons, recent case counts carried nearly all the skill. Climate, remote sensing, population, the embeddings and the control together moved the WIS relative to the seasonal baseline by at most 0.01 in every country (Table S17). <!-- src: S1_baselines.csv rel_wis_vs_L0 LightGBM L1 vs L4+C* (BR 0.362/0.357, CO 0.375/0.375, PE 0.370/0.373, TW 0.428/0.436) --> Any slowly varying descriptor of place had little room to help, so this analysis asks whether embeddings add skill to a realistic short-term model, not whether they contain information about dengue risk.

### 4.2 Why the embeddings hurt where they do

Annual embeddings differ between districts but not within a season, so a tree ensemble can use them mainly to shift a district's forecast level. Our results are consistent with such shifts harming forecasts of weeks without cases, where the embeddings raised the upper quantiles in every cell (Section 3.5). <!-- src: T5_strata.csv G_excess zero_filled contrib_pct, contrib_p (13/13 negative, p < 0.05 in 11); S6_q90_mechanism.csv dq90_log, dq90_log_p (TW_S1 p 0.15) -->

Part of the loss is generic: a shuffled embedding of the same size cost one-third to four-fifths as much as the real embeddings (Section 3.6). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv C3 G_id_pct / G_emb_pct: colombia −0.12/−0.36 = 0.33, peru −0.76/−0.94 = 0.81, taiwan −0.59/−0.94 = 0.63 (Taiwan without 2021) --> The AlphaEarth model was also trained to reconstruct climate reanalysis and elevation [@brown2025alphaearth], so part of the embeddings' content may duplicate our climate and remote-sensing inputs. <!-- src: Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 (ERA5-Land and GLO-30 DEM as training targets), read 2026-11-18 -->

Model capacity matters too: it changed where the loss appeared (Section 3.5), and in Brazil the gap between the shared configuration and per-model tuning was about eight times the embedding gain (Supplementary Methods, section C). Comparisons that tune each input set separately partly measure the tuning. <!-- src: S1 section C (T2_ladder_long.csv: 0.00327/0.00043 = 7.7 pooled h{1,4}) --> The losses do not depend on the larger trees: five of the six cells with a loss used 31-leaf configurations. <!-- src: docs/methods/split_pipeline.md "Params" (Colombia 31/100 for CO_S1–CO_S4; Colombia's configuration for BR_S5; Brazil's 127/100 for TW_S5) -->

Taiwan held out is a stress test. Nearly all its rows were zero-filled, its embeddings lay far from the training districts', and the embeddings raised its zero-row 0.9 quantile by 0.099 log units. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv TW_S5 zero_filled row_share 0.98; S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 cos_max_mean 0.70–0.82; S6_q90_mechanism.csv TW_S5 dq90_log --> This fits a shift in embedding space, but the loss did not grow with distance: every township lost (Section 3.7). <!-- src: S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 G_excess_share_units_neg = 1 in every quintile (285/285, with 2021); S7_embedding_distance.csv TW_S5 G_excess cos_max spearman_rho --> Taiwan's case definition (local cases only), surveillance and sparse counts may also contribute. Models trained elsewhere beat the in-country reference model for Taiwan and Peru, so the loss arose with the embeddings, not with transfer. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv WIS_L3 TW_S5 0.0202 vs TW_S1 0.0312; PE_S5 0.2362 vs PE_S1 0.2418 --> Only with Peru held out did gains rise appreciably with similarity (Spearman correlation 0.47; Table S39), and that estimate came from outbreak weeks and reversed under weighting. <!-- src: S7_embedding_distance.csv G_excess cos_max spearman_rho (PE_S5 +0.47; others −0.13 to +0.15); T1_primary_family.csv PE_S5; S3_outbreak.csv PE_S5; S4_weighted.csv PE_S5 --> Similarity to the training set, a check of the model's area of applicability [@meyer2021aoa], is thus a useful but insufficient screen.

### 4.3 Forecasts without case history

Without case history (Brazil, new years), coordinates reduced forecast error by 3.3%, the embeddings alone gave an uncertain +2.5%, and beyond coordinates the embeddings added no detectable gain (Section 3.8). <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_id, G_emb, G_excess pct_of_ref, lo95_pct, hi95_pct --> This does not meet the pre-specified identifier criterion, because the intervals of both the embedding gain and the excess gain are too wide (minimum detectable excess gain 2.3%). <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_excess mde_pct; docs/methods/nohist_arm.md "Interpretation" -->

Coordinates are not a pure identifier, because they follow climate zones and biomes, but at the 2019 origin a district identifier helped almost as much, random smooth fields somewhat less, and a shuffled embedding hurt (Section 3.8). <!-- src: T4_cstar_selection.csv BR_S1_nohist wis_log_2019 (N3 0.3543; +C2 0.3463; +C6 0.3472; +C4 0.3490; +C3 0.3596) --> The benefit of location therefore came from identity or smooth geography rather than from any embedding-shaped code, and the embeddings added no detectable skill beyond coordinates. This fits embeddings acting as a location proxy, but it cannot separate that role from environmental information that coordinates also carry. Results for this analysis's pre-specified main design, the held-out-states split, where an identifier cannot transfer, and for the identifier panel are [pending]. <!-- PENDING: BR S2 no-history; no-history C6 test years (RH): rewrite this paragraph after the fill --> Even a gain there would not identify an environmental mechanism.

### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

Our results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used a different embedding, built partly from human activity data, monthly targets and a residual adjustment [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary]. They do suggest that small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control before they are read as place information. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we found no embedding advantage beyond location. A gain that vanishes once coordinates are added appears in our analysis without case history and, as point estimates, in the Brazilian new-years control panel (+0.29% without a control, +0.02% with coordinates). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29, C2 G_excess_pct 0.02; T1_primary_family.csv C_star --> They also fit evidence that location proxies serve interpolation better than extrapolation [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies] and that embedding-location confounding depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite]. AlphaEarth gains reported for annual malaria and survey-based outcomes [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories, closer to our analysis without case history.

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

Strengths include four countries, fixed rows and capacity across the ladder, several location controls, equivalence testing, independent checks of filled zeros and a dated deviation log. The null result held under weighting, recalibration, the case scale and all rows, with two nominal, unadjusted gains (Colombia held out with case weights, +0.49%; Peru held out on all rows, +1.01%). <!-- src: S4_weighted.csv (CO_S5 pre_cases_plus1 +0.49, p 0.026); S5_recalibration.csv; T1_primary_family.csv case_pct, case_p, unfiltered_pct, unfiltered_p (PE_S5 +1.01, p 0.046) -->

Among the limitations, final, revised counts make absolute skill optimistic, and a two-week reporting delay only approximates real time. Because AlphaEarth starts in 2017, the 2020 models learned from two years only, and the protocol's longer training window was not run. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 35 --> The AlphaEarth model was trained on imagery extending into our test years [@brown2025alphaearth], which is expected to favour the embeddings. <!-- src: R8_literature.md section 4; R10_fact_check.md row 2; docs/data/completeness_v1.md "Other exclusion files" --> Colombia's losses arise mainly in filled weeks, which, as in Peru, were validated only in aggregate, <!-- src: T5_strata.csv CO_S1–CO_S4 zero_filled contrib_pct (−0.59 to −0.76) vs total_pct (−0.39 to −0.65) --> and Espírito Santo's false zeros remain in the training targets. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34 -->

Held-out districts were only partly unseen (Sections 2.6 and 2.7). Tuning within each fold would have chosen smaller trees in 12 of 15 Brazilian folds (validation cost 0.11% to 1.05%), and we did not rerun these cells. <!-- src: RF_summary.md --> We tested district means of one embedding with one model class and configuration; another learner, aggregation or tuning budget might use the embeddings better. ### 4.6 Implications and future work

We found no evidence of benefit, but small gains are not excluded: the upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil (new years) and +2.05% with Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> We make no recommendation about operational use. Follow-up work should compare PDFM embeddings for Brazil, which require approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm]; test a second model class and per-model tuning with an equal budget; extend to longer horizons; run the planned monthly replication; compare year-lagged with static embeddings; evaluate on data versions with realistic reporting delays; and study districts with sparse or partial surveillance.

### 4.7 Conclusions

Across four countries, AlphaEarth embeddings added no detectable skill to weekly district dengue forecasts beyond surveillance history and location controls. In 8 of 13 cells, gains or losses larger than 1% were excluded [pending]. <!-- PENDING: BR S4 and final Holm m=14: recheck "8 of 13" --> <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/13) --> The embeddings reduced accuracy slightly in several settings, and by a large fraction of a small baseline error when models were transferred to Taiwan, where nearly all weeks have no cases. In Brazil without case history, coordinates improved forecasts, while the embeddings added no detectable skill alone or beyond coordinates, a pattern that fits a location proxy. Before gains from foundation-model features are attributed to information about place, they should be tested against location-only controls, at fixed model capacity, on held-out places, against a stated equivalence margin and with a similarity check before transfer.
