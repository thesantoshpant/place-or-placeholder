### 4.1 Principal findings

With case history in the model, AlphaEarth embeddings did not detectably improve weekly district dengue forecasts in any of the 14 comparisons, with or without a location control. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, Holm_p (m = 14), G_emb_pct, G_emb_lo95_pct, G_emb_hi95_pct --> Of six losses after correction, only the small one in Colombia's held-out states held up under both stricter checks and the "no control" rule; the others, including the loss with Taiwan held out (absolute 0.0037), depended on the control or the check. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv B4_Holm_p, p_iaaft_Holm (both < 0.05 only CO_S2; BR_S5 B4 0.095); BR_S5 G_emb_p 0.39; TW_S5 G_excess −0.00369; R3_A2_nocontrol_rule.csv CO_S2 Holm_p 0.0009; R3_C_seeds.csv CO_S2 -->

In unseen states, the embeddings predicted which districts carry a high dengue burden, less well than standard covariates but far better than coordinates. In Brazil and Colombia, this information largely duplicated what static covariates (with coordinates) already provide, even before case history was added (added R² about 0.01; Section 3.9). <!-- src: R3_E1_info_probe.md T1 LightGBM grouped-state CV: E 0.49/0.52/0.24/0.45; S 0.65/0.66/0.43/0.55; ΔR² E over G+S BR +0.010, CO +0.010; PE −0.071 and TW +0.070 with CIs including zero -->

At one and four weeks ahead, recent cases carry nearly all the forecasting skill. The design detected known covariates only in Brazil and partly in Colombia (Section 3.1), so the null results in Peru and Taiwan say little. <!-- src: R3_A1_positive_control.csv BR_S1 L1->L2 0.57, L2->L3 0.52; CO_S1 L2->L3 0.30 -->

### 4.2 Why the embeddings can make forecasts worse

Most rows are weeks we filled with zero, and the losses concentrate there. In Colombia, however, the loss persisted at about half to four-fifths of its size in district-years with reported cases (Table S48), so it is not only an artefact of filled zeros. <!-- src: R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv CO_S1, CO_S2, CO_S4 case district-years p < 0.01 (max 0.0084); Results 3.5 --> We did not analyse how the trees use the embeddings; the following are hypotheses. Annual embeddings do not change within a season, so the model probably uses them mainly to move its forecasts up or down. They raised the upper forecast quantiles on zero weeks everywhere (Section 3.5). <!-- src: S6_q90_mechanism.csv dq90_log > 0 in 14/14; R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv zero-week width 14/14 (significant in 13), case-week coverage PE_S5 +2.1 pp (week-weighted, Table S48) --> Such a shift loses on zero weeks and gains on case weeks whatever its overall merit [@lerch2017dilemma].

Part of the loss seems to come simply from adding many columns. In Colombia, Peru and Taiwan, shuffled embeddings of the same size cost one-third to four-fifths as much as the real ones; in Brazil they cost almost nothing, while the real ones gained slightly (Section 3.6). Compact summaries also shrank the loss (Section 3.9). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv C3 G_id_pct / G_emb_pct: colombia 0.34, peru 0.81; R3_C_tw2021_panel.csv taiwan 0.58/1.06 = 0.55; R3_C_variants.csv V1, V2 --> Sixty-four columns that barely change within a district probably give the trees many weak, correlated inputs to split on, whatever the columns contain. A static embedding did not remove the loss, so year-to-year noise is not the whole explanation. AlphaEarth was trained partly to reconstruct climate reanalysis and elevation [@brown2025alphaearth], which may explain why the embeddings repeat information already in the climate and remote-sensing inputs. <!-- src: R3_C_variants.csv V3, V4; Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 (ERA5-Land and GLO-30 DEM as training targets), read 2026-11-18 -->

Tree size also matters. In Brazil, tuning each model separately changed WIS by about eight times the embedding gain (Supplementary Methods, section C), so comparisons that tune each input set separately partly measure the tuning. <!-- src: S1 section C (T2_ladder_long.csv: 0.00327/0.00043 = 7.7 pooled h{1,4}); R3_A5_planned_vs_reported.csv PE_S1 per-level +0.65 --> Tuning does not explain the Colombian loss (Section 3.9). <!-- src: R3_C_tuning.csv -->

Taiwan held out is a stress test: 98% of its rows were zero-filled, and its embeddings lay far from those of the training districts. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv TW_S5 zero_filled row_share 0.98; S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 cos_max_mean 0.70–0.82; S6_q90_mechanism.csv TW_S5 dq90_log 0.0929 --> Every township lost, whatever its similarity to the training districts [@meyer2021aoa], mostly in district-years without cases. <!-- src: S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 G_excess_share_units_neg = 1; R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv TW_S5 −3.28 on case district-years vs −18.3 --> One possible explanation is that embeddings differ systematically between countries, so a model can use them to absorb each country's case definition and reporting level. A level learned from the other countries would be wrong for every Taiwanese township, because Taiwan counts only locally acquired confirmed cases. This explanation fits the embedding gain more directly than the excess gain. Both models of the excess gain contain shuffled embeddings, and because these are drawn within each country, they already mark the country. We did not test embeddings centred within each country, which would remove this route. Dengue is also not endemic in Taiwan, where imported cases can start local epidemics and local cases are concentrated in Tainan, Kaohsiung and Pingtung in the south [@shang2010imported]. <!-- src: Shang et al. 2010, PLoS Negl Trop Dis 4(8):e775: "non-endemic areas such as Taiwan" (Discussion); "imported dengue cases are able to initiate indigenous epidemics" (Abstract, Conclusions); "98.45% (9,756/9,910) of total confirmed indigenous cases in Taiwan" were in Tainan, Kaohsiung and Pingtung, 1998–2007 (Results); https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0000775 (full text read via Europe PMC, PMC2914757), accessed 2026-11-21 --> Environmental similarity to endemic districts therefore says little about local risk.

### 4.3 Forecasts without case history

Without case history, coordinates and a district label both reduced error in Brazil's future years, and the embeddings added nothing detectable beyond either. In held-out states, where a label cannot help, nothing was detected, but intervals were wide. <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_id 3.30, G_excess −0.43; docs/results/review/RH_nohist_c6.md (C6 G_id +1.98; G_excess with C6 −0.04); BR_S2_nohist pooled G_id +0.88 [−0.48, 2.23], G_emb +1.52 [−1.27, 4.32], G_excess −0.01 [−1.28, 1.27], mde_pct 1.80 --> The future-years pattern is consistent with coordinates and embeddings acting partly as a location label, but the criterion we specified in advance was met in neither split. <!-- src: docs/methods/nohist_arm.md "Interpretation"; FINAL_RESULTS_BRIEF.md section 4 --> Other results argue against a pure labelling role. Shuffled embeddings, which identify districts as stably as the real ones, did not help in Brazil's future years (Table S36) or at the 2019 selection without case history (Table S22). In Brazil, a district's embedding also changes from year to year about as much as it differs from its neighbours (Table S49). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil C3 G_id −0.04; T4_cstar_selection.csv BR_S1_nohist N3 0.35428 vs C3 0.35963; R3_A7_embedding_stability.csv BR 0.982 vs 0.974 --> <!-- src: (Colombia sentence cut in v1g; Section 3.8) R3_C_co_s2_nohist.csv --> Coordinates also follow climate zones, which makes identity hard to separate from environment. These analyses are exploratory, with one seed, settings tuned with case history and no shuffled-embedding run in the test years.

### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

Our results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used another embedding, monthly targets and a residual design [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary], but small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we found no detectable embedding advantage, with or without location inputs. In Brazil the point estimate moved from +0.29% (seed 0) without a control to +0.02% with coordinates, a change well within the uncertainty. <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29 (seed 0), C2 G_excess_pct 0.02 --> Our results also agree with evidence that stand-ins for location help more within the training area than beyond it [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies], and that how far embeddings stand in for location depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite]. AlphaEarth gains for malaria and respiratory infection [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories.

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

The study's strengths are four countries, fixed scored forecasts and model settings, several location controls, a positive control, equivalence tests and a dated log of protocol changes. We could not test PDFM, the embedding used in both earlier dengue studies, because it requires approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm]; our results apply to AlphaEarth only.

Every model used the same LightGBM settings, tuned without embeddings over a small grid. This removes tuning as a confound but may not suit 64 extra correlated inputs. In Brazil, separate tuning favoured smaller trees, most strongly for the embedding model. Peru's future-years estimate changed sign under separate tuning. The held-out-country models, including Taiwan's, used Brazil's settings (Colombia's when Brazil was held out), which were not tuned for pooled data. In a sensitivity analysis, only Colombia's future years were tuned on a wider grid for both models. <!-- src: S1_supplement_methods.md section C "Per-level tuning" (L4 gap 1.36%) and "Tuning" (S5 settings); Table S43 PE_S1 per-level +0.65; Table S55 --> The embeddings also entered as 64 raw inputs. They are designed for linear and nearest-neighbour use, and because their axes are arbitrary, tree splits may use them inefficiently. A nearest-neighbour risk feature and eight principal components, which suit that design better, also gave no gain (Section 3.9). Other learners, residual designs and learned location encoders [@russwurm2024location; @vivanco2023geoclip; @klemmer2025satclip] may behave differently.

We used one snapshot of final, revised counts. In practice the latest weeks are incomplete when a forecast is made, so all models would be less accurate in real time, and place inputs might matter more under delayed reporting. Shifting the case inputs back by two weeks in Brazil's future years gave an embedding gain of +0.60% (95% CI −0.56 to 1.76; Table S37), but this does not reproduce real reporting delays. <!-- src: T6a_sensitivities.csv Brazil S1 delay2 G_emb (Table S37: +0.60, −0.56 to 1.76, p 0.29) --> Losses arose mainly on zero-filled weeks. These were checked against independent district-level data only in Brazil and Taiwan, so some filled zeros in Colombia and Peru may hide unreported cases, although the Colombian loss persisted in district-years with cases. The zeros we filled for Espírito Santo remain in the training targets. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34; S1_supplement_methods.md A4, A6 --> Test years 2020–2021 fell in the COVID-19 pandemic, when dengue notification declined in parts of Latin America [@chen2022covid; @roster2024covidbrazil]. AlphaEarth was trained on imagery that overlaps our test years [@brown2025alphaearth], which, if anything, favours the embeddings. <!-- src: R8_literature.md section 4 (Brown et al. §S15, §S22.2); R10_fact_check.md row 2 -->

Every model, with or without embeddings, was overconfident in weeks with cases. The full model's 80% intervals contained only 61% to 75% of those weeks (23% in Taiwan's future years), and embeddings did not fix this. <!-- src: Table S20 cov_80_nonzero L4 + C*; Table S18; S1_supplement_methods.md section I (other ladder steps 56–75%) -->

Training began in 2018 because the embeddings begin in 2017, so inputs that are constant within a year had few district-years to learn from in early test years. The planned longer window for the models without embeddings was not run. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 35 --> Held-out districts were only partly unseen (Section 2.7). Had tree size been tuned within each fold, smaller trees would have been chosen in 12 of 15 Brazilian folds. <!-- src: RF_summary.md --> Removing all case history does not mimic sparse or delayed surveillance.

When the difference between models drifted across seasons, the main test gave too many false positives. On the final rows, the surrogate-series check put its size at 14% to 18% at a nominal 5% in five comparisons (Table S47). An earlier simulation, run before the origin-year filter, gave 10% to 22% (Table S12). <!-- src: R3_A8_iaaft_equivalence.csv size_nominal5, method ewc_hln: BR_S1 18.4, BR_S2 14.0, BR_S3 15.2, BR_S4 14.9, CO_S5 15.8 (Table S47); Table S12 IAAFT worst comparisons 0.10–0.22 --> In the loss comparisons we checked, the false-positive rate was close to the nominal level (1% to 9%). <!-- src: R3_A8_iaaft_equivalence.csv BR_S5 6.5, CO_S1 8.7, CO_S2 1.1, CO_S4 7.35 --> Our conclusions do not extend to other seasons, and with Peru or Taiwan held out only the excess gain has three seeds. <!-- src: R3_C_summary.md section 3 --> Some decisions, including the ±1% margin, one set of settings per country and Taiwan's 2021, followed results (Section 2.12). Further limitations are in Supplementary Methods, section I.

### 4.6 Implications and future work

We cannot rule out small gains. Upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil's future years, +0.71% in its held-out districts and +2.02% with Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, BR_S4, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> In our data, recent case counts carried nearly all of the forecast skill, and AlphaEarth embeddings added none we could detect. We did not evaluate operational use. Follow-up work will test PDFM in Brazil, other model designs (a second model class, a residual design on zero-shot TimesFM, embeddings centred within each country) and more realistic settings (longer horizons, monthly targets, reporting delays).

### 4.7 Conclusions

Across four countries, AlphaEarth embeddings carried information about where dengue burden is high; in Brazil and Colombia, it largely duplicated standard covariates. With case history, they added no detectable accuracy to weekly district dengue forecasts. Effects larger than ±1%, about Brazil's combined gain from all non-case inputs, were ruled out in 8 of 14 comparisons, or 5 under the strict-variance check. <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/14); R3_A4_equivalence.csv B4 Holm 5/14 --> With Taiwan held out, whose case definition differs from that of the training countries, embeddings worsened forecasts, though not robustly. Without case history, our results do not settle whether embeddings act as a location label. A gain from foundation-model features should not be credited to place until it has been tested against location-only controls in places left out of training. The test should use the same model settings throughout and a margin stated in advance.
