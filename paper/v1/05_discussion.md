### 4.1 Principal findings

With surveillance history in the model, we found no evidence that AlphaEarth embeddings improve weekly district dengue forecasts. In answer to our questions: no cell showed a gain in new years (RQ1); location controls added no detectable skill, and no embedding gain was left to attribute to identity (RQ2); no gain appeared in unseen districts, states or countries, and embeddings harmed transfer to Taiwan (RQ3); losses arose mainly in weeks without cases, and gains, where present, in weeks with cases (RQ4, exploratory). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, Holm_p_available, G_id_pct, G_id_lo95_pct; T5_strata.csv G_excess contrib_pct by stratum -->

No excess gain survived correction [[PENDING: BR S4]] (Holm over the 13 available cells; final m = 14 [[PENDING: final Holm m=14]]), and in 8 of 13 cells differences larger than ±1% of forecast error were excluded after adjustment. <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/13) --> Six cells showed losses after correction, five small and one large relative to a low baseline (Taiwan held out; absolute 0.0035). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, G_excess, Holm_p_available, loss_or_gain --> Under more cautious variance estimates, two of these losses (Brazil held out; Colombia, held-out states) remained significant after correction under both checks. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv B4_Holm_p, p_iaaft_Holm (BR_S5 0.027/0.0065; CO_S2 0.028/0.0065) --> With case history the history already identifies the district, so place versus placeholder cannot be settled in this arm.

The null is not specific to embeddings. At one- and four-week horizons, recent case counts carried nearly all the skill: climate, remote sensing, population, embeddings and the control together moved relative WIS against the seasonal baseline by at most 0.01 in every country (Supplementary Table S2-1). <!-- src: S1_baselines.csv rel_wis_vs_L0 LightGBM L1 vs L4+C* (BR 0.362/0.357, CO 0.375/0.375, PE 0.370/0.373, TW 0.428/0.436) --> Any slowly varying descriptor of place had little room to help, so the history arm asks whether embeddings add skill to a realistic short-term model, not whether they contain information about dengue risk; longer horizons and places with sparse surveillance are where such information could matter more.

### 4.2 Why the embeddings hurt where they do

Annual embeddings differ between districts but not within a season, so a tree ensemble can use them mainly to shift a district's forecast level. Our measurements are consistent with such shifts harming forecasts of silent weeks, where the embedding models raised the upper quantiles in every cell (Results). <!-- src: T5_strata.csv G_excess zero_filled contrib_pct, contrib_p (13/13 negative, p < 0.05 in 11); S6_q90_mechanism.csv dq90_log, dq90_log_p (TW_S1 p 0.15) -->

Part of the loss is generic. A shuffled embedding of the same size, placed on the wrong district, cost one-third to four-fifths as much as the real embeddings (Colombia, Peru and Taiwan, new years), so the harm is partly dimensionality and partly specific. <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv C3 G_id_pct / G_emb_pct: colombia −0.12/−0.36 = 0.33, peru −0.76/−0.94 = 0.81, taiwan −0.59/−0.94 = 0.63 (Taiwan without 2021) --> The embedding model was also trained to reconstruct climate reanalysis and elevation [@brown2025alphaearth], so part of its content may duplicate our climate and remote-sensing inputs. <!-- src: Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 (ERA5-Land and GLO-30 DEM as training targets), read 2026-11-18 --> Capacity changes where the loss appears (Results), and the gap between the shared and per-rung configurations was about eight times the embedding gain (Supplement S1, section D), so comparisons that tune each feature set separately partly measure the tuner. <!-- src: S1 section D (T2_ladder_long.csv: 0.00327/0.00043 = 7.7 pooled h{1,4}) --> The losses do not depend on the larger trees: five of the six loss cells used 31-leaf configurations. <!-- src: docs/methods/split_pipeline.md "Params" (Colombia 31/100 for CO_S1–CO_S4; Colombia's configuration for BR_S5; Brazil's 127/100 for TW_S5) -->

Taiwan held out is the stress test. Nearly all its rows were zero-filled, its embeddings lay far from the training districts', and the embedding model raised its zero-row 0.9 quantile by 0.099 log units. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv TW_S5 zero_filled row_share 0.98; S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 cos_max_mean 0.70–0.82; S6_q90_mechanism.csv TW_S5 dq90_log --> This is consistent with a shift in embedding space, but the loss did not grow with distance: every township lost (Results). <!-- src: S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 G_excess_share_units_neg = 1 in every quintile (285/285, with 2021); S7_embedding_distance.csv TW_S5 G_excess cos_max spearman_rho --> Taiwan's case definition (local cases only), surveillance and sparse counts may also contribute. Transfer itself worked (models trained elsewhere beat in-country L3 for Taiwan and Peru), so the loss arose with the embeddings. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv WIS_L3 TW_S5 0.0202 vs TW_S1 0.0312; PE_S5 0.2362 vs PE_S1 0.2418 --> Only with Peru held out did gains rise appreciably with similarity (ρ = 0.47; |ρ| ≤ 0.15 elsewhere), and its positive estimate came from outbreak weeks and reversed under weighting. <!-- src: S7_embedding_distance.csv G_excess cos_max spearman_rho (PE_S5 +0.47; others −0.13 to +0.15); T1_primary_family.csv PE_S5; S3_outbreak.csv PE_S5; S4_weighted.csv PE_S5 --> Similarity to the training set, an area-of-applicability check [@meyer2021aoa], is thus a useful but insufficient screen.

### 4.3 Without case history

In Brazil (new years) without case history, coordinates (the selected control) reduced forecast error by 3.3% (95% CI 1.7 to 4.9). Embeddings alone gave an uncertain +2.5% (−0.8 to 5.9), and beyond coordinates they added no detectable gain (excess gain −0.43%, −2.06 to 1.19). <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_id, G_emb, G_excess pct_of_ref, lo95_pct, hi95_pct --> This does not meet the pre-specified identifier criterion, because both intervals are too wide (minimum detectable excess gain 2.3%). <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_excess mde_pct; docs/methods/nohist_arm.md "Interpretation" -->

Coordinates are not a pure identifier: they also follow climate zones and biomes. At the 2019 selection origin, a district-identifier control improved N3 almost as much as coordinates, a random smooth spatial field somewhat less, and a shuffled embedding made it worse (Supplementary Table S2-4). <!-- src: T4_cstar_selection.csv BR_S1_nohist wis_log_2019 (N3 0.3543; +C2 0.3463; +C6 0.3472; +C4 0.3490; +C3 0.3596) --> The benefit of location without case history therefore came from identity or smooth geography rather than from any embedding-shaped code, and the embeddings added no detectable skill beyond coordinates. This fits embeddings acting as a location proxy; it cannot separate that from environmental information that coordinates also carry. [[PENDING: no-history C6 test years (RH)]] The arm's pre-specified headline is the held-out-states split, where an identifier cannot transfer. [[PENDING: BR S2 no-history]] Even a gain there would not identify an environmental mechanism, and removing all history does not mimic sparse or delayed surveillance. The arm also reuses hyperparameters tuned with case history.

### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

These results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used a different embedding built partly from human activity data, monthly targets and residual adjustment [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary]. They do suggest that small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control before they are read as place information. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we find no embedding advantage beyond location: a gain that vanishes once coordinates are added appears in our no-history arm and, as point estimates, in the Brazil new-years control panel (+0.29% without a control, +0.02% with coordinates). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29, C2 G_excess_pct 0.02; T1_primary_family.csv C_star --> The results fit evidence that location proxies serve interpolation better than extrapolation [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies] and that the embedding-location confound depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite]. AlphaEarth gains reported for annual malaria and survey-based outcomes [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories, which our no-history arm resembles more closely.

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

Strengths are the four-country design, fixed rows and capacity across rungs, several kinds of location control, equivalence testing, district-level checks of filled zeros against independent sources and a dated deviation log. The null held under weighting, recalibration, the case scale and all rows, with two nominal, unadjusted gains (Colombia held out with case weights, +0.49%; Peru held out on all rows, +1.01%). <!-- src: S4_weighted.csv (CO_S5 pre_cases_plus1 +0.49, p 0.026); S5_recalibration.csv; T1_primary_family.csv case_pct, case_p, unfiltered_pct, unfiltered_p (PE_S5 +1.01, p 0.046) -->

- **Retrospective, final data.** Final revised counts make absolute skill optimistic; a two-week reporting-delay sensitivity only approximates real time. [[PENDING: sensitivities yref/delay2]]
- **Short training window.** AlphaEarth starts in 2017, so every rung trains on targets from 2018 and the 2020 models learn from two years only; the protocol's long-window comparison was not run. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 35 -->
- **Later-dated information.** The AlphaEarth model was trained on imagery extending into our test years [@brown2025alphaearth], which is expected to favour the embeddings; other later-dated information (Methods 2.6) affects all models alike. <!-- src: R8_literature.md section 4; R10_fact_check.md row 2; docs/data/completeness_v1.md "Other exclusion files" -->
- **Filled zeros.** Colombia's losses arise mainly in filled weeks, which (as in Peru) were validated only in aggregate. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv CO_S1–CO_S4 zero_filled contrib_pct (−0.59 to −0.76) vs total_pct (−0.39 to −0.65) --> False zeros in Espírito Santo were excluded from scoring but not from training. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34 -->
- **"Unseen" districts.** Held-out districts keep their lagged cases, and their pre-test outcomes informed tuning and control selection. Fold-internal tuning would choose smaller trees in 12 of 15 Brazilian folds (validation cost 0.11–1.05%); we did not rerun. <!-- src: RF_summary.md -->
- **One representation, one learner.** District means of one embedding, one model class and one shared configuration were tested; another learner, aggregation or tuning budget might use the embeddings better. Rescaling district means to unit length discards their norm (0.70–0.98), a measure of within-district heterogeneity that may carry signal. <!-- src: docs/features/alphaearth_extraction.md; R10_fact_check.md items 1b, 4b (population-weighted norm range 0.70–0.98) -->
- **Inference.** The primary test over-rejects when gains drift across seasons (10–22% at nominal 5% in surrogate simulations computed before the origin-year filter and the Espírito Santo exclusion). <!-- src: RC_summary.md size table (pre-kit-v3 rows) --> Four of the six losses were significant after correction only under some variance estimators (Results). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv B4_Holm_p, p_iaaft_Holm --> Across three seeds in Brazil (new years) the excess gain varied little (SD 2 × 10⁻⁵ against an estimate of 4 × 10⁻⁴), but single-model WIS varied by 1–3 × 10⁻⁴, and cells that use the shuffled-embedding control depend on one random assignment. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1 seed_sd 2.03e-5, G_excess 3.89e-4; docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 16 (per-horizon seed SD of single-model WIS about 0.0001–0.0003); T1 C_star C3 for BR_S5, PE_S5, TW_S5 -->
- **Decisions after results.** The ±1% margin was set after the Brazil and Peru new-years results. Eleven of the first 23 deviations followed some new-years results, none a spatial result; D24–D38 followed review, when only Brazil S4 and the no-history arm were unseen. <!-- src: RESOLUTION_LOG.md section I (REV2-Major1/2); docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md header and rows 24–38 --> The protocol and deviation log were deposited before those results were read (Methods 2.11).

Intervals were too narrow on weeks with cases (Results); calibration, country-specific limits and covariate details are in Supplement S1, section I.

### 4.6 Implications and future work

We found no evidence of benefit, but small gains are not excluded: upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil (new years) and +2.05% with Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> We make no recommendation about operational use. Follow-up work should compare PDFM embeddings for Brazil, which require approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm]; test a second model class and per-level tuning with an equal budget; extend to longer horizons, where slowly varying descriptors may matter more; run the monthly arm; compare year-lagged with static embeddings; evaluate on data vintages with realistic reporting delays; and study districts with sparse or partial surveillance.

### 4.7 Conclusion

Across four countries, AlphaEarth embeddings added no detectable skill to weekly district dengue forecasts beyond surveillance history and location controls, and in 8 of 13 settings gains or losses larger than 1% were excluded. <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/13) --> They reduced accuracy slightly in several settings, and by a large fraction of a small baseline error when transferred to Taiwan, where nearly all weeks have no cases. In Brazil without case history, coordinates improved forecasts while embeddings alone did not detectably, and the embeddings added no detectable skill beyond coordinates, a pattern that fits a location proxy. Before gains from foundation-model features are attributed to information about place, they should be tested against location-only controls, at fixed capacity, on held-out places, against a stated equivalence margin and with a similarity check before transfer.
