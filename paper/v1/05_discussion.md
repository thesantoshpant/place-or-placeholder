The embeddings are not empty: in unseen states, they predicted which districts carry a high dengue burden about as well as standard covariates and far better than coordinates. But this information duplicates what coordinates and static covariates already provide, even before case history is added (added R² about 0.01; Section 3.9). <!-- src: R3_E1_info_probe.md T1 LightGBM grouped-state CV: E 0.49/0.52/0.24/0.45; ΔR² E over G+S BR +0.010, CO +0.010 --> This is our answer to the title's question: the embeddings carry real place information, but in these four countries it is redundant.

Because case history identifies every district, no control in the main analysis tests identity, and the spatial splits removed only the chance to memorise which embedding belongs to which district.

The key limit on scope is that, at one and four weeks, recent cases carry nearly all the skill, and the design detected known covariates only in Brazil and partly in Colombia (Section 3.1); in Peru and Taiwan the nulls say little. <!-- src: R3_A1_positive_control.csv BR_S1 L1->L2 0.57, L2->L3 0.52; CO_S1 L2->L3 0.30 -->

### 4.2 Why the embeddings hurt where they do

Annual embeddings are constant within a season, so trees use them mainly to shift forecast levels. They raised upper quantiles on zero weeks in every cell and widened zero-week intervals in 12 of 13, but improved case-week coverage only with Peru held out (Section 3.5). <!-- src: S6_q90_mechanism.csv dq90_log 13/13; R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv zero-week width 12/13, case-week coverage PE_S5 +2.4 pp --> Such a shift loses on zero weeks and gains on case weeks whatever its overall merit [@lerch2017dilemma]. The Colombian loss persisted in district-years with reported cases, so it is not only an artefact of filled zeros. <!-- src: R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv CO_S1, CO_S2, CO_S4 case district-years p ≤ 0.016 -->

Part of the loss is a cost of dimensionality. A shuffled embedding of the same size cost one-third to four-fifths as much as the real one, and one- or eight-column summaries shrank the loss without reversing it (Sections 3.6 and 3.9). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv C3 G_id_pct / G_emb_pct: colombia 0.34, peru 0.81; R3_C_tw2021_panel.csv taiwan 0.58/1.06 = 0.55; R3_C_variants.csv V1, V2 --> Sixty-four columns that are nearly constant within a district give the trees many ways to split districts, whatever their content. A static embedding did not remove the loss, so year-to-year noise is not the explanation, and the embeddings could not replace hand-built remote sensing. AlphaEarth was trained partly to reconstruct climate reanalysis and elevation [@brown2025alphaearth], which may explain the overlap. <!-- src: R3_C_variants.csv V3, V4; Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 (ERA5-Land and GLO-30 DEM as training targets), read 2026-11-18 -->

Model capacity matters: in Brazil, per-model tuning changed WIS by about eight times the embedding gain, and it reversed the sign of Peru's new-years estimate (Section 3.4), so comparisons that tune each input set separately partly measure the tuning. <!-- src: S1 section C (T2_ladder_long.csv: 0.00327/0.00043 = 7.7 pooled h{1,4}); R3_A5_planned_vs_reported.csv PE_S1 per-level +0.65 --> In Colombia, separate tuning chose the shared configuration, so the Colombian loss does not reflect it. <!-- src: R3_C_tuning.csv -->

Taiwan held out is a stress test: 98% of rows were zero-filled, its embeddings lay far from the training districts', and they raised its zero-row 0.9 quantile by 0.099 log units. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv TW_S5 zero_filled row_share 0.98; S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 cos_max_mean 0.70–0.82; S6_q90_mechanism.csv TW_S5 dq90_log --> Every township lost, regardless of embedding distance, mostly in district-years without cases. <!-- src: S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 G_excess_share_units_neg = 1; R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv TW_S5 −3.36 on case district-years vs −17.3 --> A uniform shift fits embeddings that separate countries and so carry each country's case definition and reporting level (Taiwan counts only locally acquired confirmed cases). The reference model transferred well, and similarity to the training set [@meyer2021aoa] was an insufficient screen.

### 4.3 Forecasts without case history

Without case history, the two countries disagreed. In Brazil's new years, coordinates reduced error by 3.3%, a pure identifier by 2.0%, and the embeddings added nothing detectable beyond either. <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_id, G_excess; docs/results/review/RH_nohist_c6.md (C6 G_id +1.98; G_excess with C6 −0.04) --> This is consistent with the embeddings acting partly as a location label, but the pre-specified criterion was not met and the intervals were wide (minimum detectable excess gain 2.3%). <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_excess mde_pct; docs/methods/nohist_arm.md "Interpretation" --> In Colombia's held-out states, coordinates, static history and the embeddings all made forecasts worse. <!-- src: R3_C_co_s2_nohist.csv --> Coordinates also follow climate zones, so they cannot separate identity from environment. ### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

Our results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used another embedding, monthly targets and a residual design [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary], but small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we found no embedding advantage beyond location; in Brazil, the point estimate fell from +0.29% without a control to +0.02% with coordinates. <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29 (seed 0), C2 G_excess_pct 0.02 --> They also fit evidence that location proxies serve interpolation better than extrapolation [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies], that embedding-location confounding depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite], and that generic image features predict many cross-sectional outcomes [@rolf2021mosaiks]. AlphaEarth gains for annual malaria and survey outcomes [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories.

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

Strengths include four countries, fixed rows and capacity, several location controls, a positive control, equivalence testing and a dated deviation log. The main external-validity gap is PDFM, the embedding used in both earlier dengue studies, which requires approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm]; our results apply to AlphaEarth only. Other learners, a residual design and learned location encoders [@vivanco2023geoclip; @klemmer2025satclip] may behave differently.

Final, revised counts flatter absolute skill. The planned longer training window was not run. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 35 --> Brazilian gains rose by test year as training grew from two to six years, but no trend was detectable, and the shortest windows were COVID-19 years. <!-- src: R3_A3_trend.csv (smallest scaled p 0.096) --> AlphaEarth was trained on imagery overlapping our test years [@brown2025alphaearth], which can only favour the embeddings. <!-- src: R8_literature.md section 4 (Brown et al. §S15, §S22.2); R10_fact_check.md row 2 --> Espírito Santo's false zeros remain in the training targets. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34 -->

Held-out districts were only partly unseen (Section 2.7); within-fold tuning would have chosen smaller trees in 12 of 15 Brazilian folds. <!-- src: RF_summary.md --> Removing all history does not mimic sparse or delayed surveillance.

The primary test rejected too often under seasonal drift (14% to 18% at a nominal 5% in four cells), though it was conservative in the cells with losses. <!-- src: R3_A8_iaaft_equivalence.csv size (EWC) BR_S1 18.4, BR_S2 14.0, BR_S3 15.2, CO_S5 15.8; loss cells ≤ 6.8 --> Inference does not extend to other seasons, and Peru and Taiwan held out have three seeds only for the excess gain. <!-- src: R3_C_summary.md section 3 --> Some decisions, including the ±1% margin, one configuration per country and Taiwan's 2021, followed results (Section 2.12). Further limitations are in Supplementary Methods, section I.

### 4.6 Implications and future work

Small gains are not excluded: upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil (new years) and +2.05% with Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> <!-- REVISIT final kit (3-seed, m=14) --> We make no recommendation about operational use. Follow-up work will test PDFM for Brazil, a second model class, a residual design on zero-shot TimesFM, embeddings centred within each country, separate tuning without case history, longer horizons, monthly targets and realistic reporting delays.

### 4.7 Conclusions

Across four countries, AlphaEarth embeddings carried real information about where dengue burden is high, but it duplicated coordinates and standard covariates. With case history, they added no detectable skill to weekly district dengue forecasts, so whether they act as place information or as an identifier did not arise. Effects beyond ±1%, about Brazil's combined benefit of all non-case inputs, were excluded in 8 of 13 cells, or 6 with a cautious variance [pending]. <!-- PENDING: BR S4, final Holm m=14 --> <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/13); R3_A4_equivalence.csv B4 Holm 6/13 --> <!-- REVISIT final kit (3-seed, m=14) --> Embeddings may worsen transfer to a country with different surveillance. Without case history, results differed between Brazil and Colombia; Brazil's are consistent with, but do not establish, a location proxy. Gains from foundation-model features should be tested against location-only controls, at fixed capacity, on held-out places and against a stated margin before they are attributed to place.
