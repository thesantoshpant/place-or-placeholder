### 4.1 Principal findings

With surveillance history in the model, we found no evidence that AlphaEarth embeddings improve weekly district dengue forecasts: no cell showed an excess gain that survived correction [[PENDING: BR S4]] (Holm over the 13 available cells; final m = 14 [[PENDING: final Holm m=14]]). Outside transfer to Taiwan, the excess gain ranged from −0.65% to +0.96% of forecast error. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref (min CO_S3 −0.654, max PE_S5 +0.960; TW_S5 excluded) --> Six cells showed losses significant after correction: five small (−0.39% to −0.65%; Brazil held out and Colombia under new years, held-out states, buffered blocks and random districts) and one large (Taiwan held out, −17.3% of a low baseline, an absolute 0.0035 in weighted interval score on log(1 + cases)). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, G_excess, Holm_p_available, loss_or_gain --> The direction of all six losses was consistent across checks, but their significance after correction depended on the variance estimator in Colombia (new years, buffered blocks, random districts) and Taiwan held out (Section 4.5). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv B4_Holm_p, p_iaaft_Holm --> The 90% interval lay within ±1% in nine cells, and in eight after Holm adjustment. <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_unadjusted (9/13), within_Holm (8/13) --> No identity gain was significantly positive. In Colombia held out the selected control made forecasts worse (identity gain −0.81%), which is why that cell's excess gain is positive (+0.22%) while its embedding gain is not (−0.04%). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv G_id_pct, G_id_p (all cells); CO_S5 G_id_pct, pct_of_WIS_ref, G_emb_pct --> With case history present, the history already identifies the district: the identity share was not estimable (and would be descriptive only, since control and embedding effects can interact in a nonlinear model), and place versus placeholder cannot be settled in this arm.

### 4.2 Why the embeddings hurt where they do

Annual embeddings differ between districts but not within a season, so a tree ensemble can use them mainly to shift a district's forecast level. Our measurements are consistent with such shifts harming forecasts of silent weeks. ### 4.3 Without case history

Without case history, an identifier and place information can be told apart. In Brazil (new years), coordinates (the selected control) reduced forecast error by 3.3% (95% CI 1.7 to 4.9). Embeddings alone gave an uncertain +2.5% (−0.8 to 5.9), and beyond coordinates they added no detectable gain (excess gain −0.43%, −2.06 to 1.19). <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_id, G_emb, G_excess pct_of_ref, lo95_pct, hi95_pct --> This is the pattern expected of a placeholder, but it does not meet the pre-specified identifier criterion (a significant embedding gain together with an excess gain shown to lie within ±1%), because both intervals are too wide (minimum detectable excess gain 2.3%); for the same reason the identity share is not estimable. <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_excess mde_pct, ID_share reason (G_emb 95% CI includes 0); docs/methods/nohist_arm.md "Interpretation" --> The arm's pre-specified headline is the held-out-states split, where an identifier cannot transfer. [[PENDING: BR S2 no-history]] Even a gain there would not identify an environmental mechanism, and removing all history does not mimic sparse, partial or delayed surveillance. The arm was specified before its runs were evaluated (commit `bd3ba50`) but after the history-arm results, and it reuses hyperparameters tuned on a model with case history.

With case history, the fixed control panel agreed. In Brazil (new years, seed 0), the non-significant embedding gain of +0.29% shrank to +0.02% once coordinates were in the model (+0.25% with historical risk); in Colombia, Peru and Taiwan no control let the embeddings help (excess gains −0.13% to −0.86%). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29, C2 G_excess_pct 0.02, C1 G_excess_pct 0.25; colombia/peru/taiwan G_excess_pct range −0.13 to −0.86 (seed 0; Taiwan without 2021) --> [[PENDING: BR C3/C6 panel]]

### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

These results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used a different embedding built partly from human activity data, monthly targets and residual adjustment [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary]. They do suggest that small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control before they are read as place information. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we find no embedding advantage beyond location; the agreement is in the conclusion rather than the mechanism, because coordinates were the selected control in only one history-arm cell (Colombia held out). <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv C_star --> The results fit evidence from spatial machine learning that location proxies serve interpolation better than extrapolation [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies] and that the embedding-location confound depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite]. AlphaEarth gains reported for annual malaria and survey-based outcomes [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories, which our no-history arm resembles more closely. In Brazil, yearly estimates were lowest in 2021, amid COVID-era disruption of transmission and reporting [@chen2022covid; @roster2024covidbrazil], and highest in the 2024 epidemic (+0.88%, not significant) [@gurgelgoncalves2024brazil]. <!-- src: S8_per_year.csv BR_S1 G_excess 2021 (−0.67) and 2024 (+0.88, p .27) -->

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

Strengths are the four-country design, fixed rows and capacity across rungs, several kinds of location control, equivalence testing, independent checks of the filled zeros and a dated log of every protocol deviation. The main result (no evidence of a gain) held under population and case weighting, causal recalibration, the case scale and scoring of all rows, although two of these exploratory estimates were nominally positive without multiplicity adjustment (Colombia held out with case weights, +0.49%; Peru held out on all rows, +1.01%, p = 0.046). <!-- src: S4_weighted.csv (CO_S5 pre_cases_plus1 +0.49, p 0.026); S5_recalibration.csv; T1_primary_family.csv case_pct, case_p, unfiltered_pct, unfiltered_p (PE_S5 +1.01, p 0.046) --> The limitations follow.

- **Retrospective, final data.** Final revised counts make absolute skill optimistic; a two-week reporting-delay sensitivity only approximates real time. [[PENDING: sensitivities yref/delay2]]

- **Filled zeros.** Brazil and Taiwan were checked district by district (in Brazil 2024, 1.5% of absent weeks held one to three probable cases), Colombia and Peru only in aggregate. <!-- src: docs/data/zero_fill_crosscheck_2024.md (excluding Espírito Santo) --> False zeros in Espírito Santo from 2020 were excluded from scoring (1.41% of Brazilian rows) but not from training. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34; KIT3_changes.md --> Taiwan's crosswalk was inferred from counts, so count agreement is not independent; a name check matched all 287 townships. <!-- src: R1_timing_leakage.md section 4 -->

### 4.6 Implications and future work

Evaluations of embedding features should include location-only controls, fixed capacity, held-out places, an equivalence margin and a similarity check before transfer. We found no evidence of benefit, but small gains are not excluded: upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil (new years) and +2.05% in Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> We make no recommendation about operational use. Follow-up work should compare PDFM embeddings for Brazil, which require approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm]; test a second model class; run the monthly arm; compare year-lagged with static embeddings; evaluate on data vintages with realistic reporting delays; and study districts with sparse or partial surveillance.

### 4.7 Conclusion

Across four countries, AlphaEarth embeddings added no detectable skill to weekly district dengue forecasts beyond surveillance history and location controls. They reduced accuracy slightly in several within-country and cross-country settings, and substantially when transferred to a dissimilar country; the direction of these losses was consistent, but four of them were significant only under some variance estimators. Without case history, plain coordinates did at least as well as the embeddings. Before gains from foundation-model features are attributed to information about place, they should be tested against location-only controls, at fixed capacity, on held-out places and against a stated equivalence margin.
