### 4.1 Principal findings

With case history in the model, AlphaEarth embeddings did not detectably improve weekly district dengue forecasts in any of the 14 comparisons, with or without a location control. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv pct_of_WIS_ref, Holm_p (m = 14), G_emb_pct, G_emb_lo95_pct, G_emb_hi95_pct --> Of six losses after correction, only the small one in Colombia's held-out states held up under both stricter checks and the "no control" rule; the others, including the loss with Taiwan held out (absolute 0.0037), depended on the control or the check. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv B4_Holm_p, p_iaaft_Holm (both < 0.05 only CO_S2; BR_S5 B4 0.095); BR_S5 G_emb_p 0.39; TW_S5 G_excess −0.00369; R3_A2_nocontrol_rule.csv CO_S2 Holm_p 0.0009; R3_C_seeds.csv CO_S2 -->

In unseen states, the embeddings predicted which districts carry a high dengue burden, less well than standard covariates but far better than coordinates. In Brazil and Colombia, this information largely duplicated what coordinates and static covariates already provide, even before case history was added (added R² about 0.01; Section 3.9). In Peru and Taiwan the added R² was too uncertain to judge. <!-- src: R3_E1_info_probe.md T1 LightGBM grouped-state CV: E 0.49/0.52/0.24/0.45; S 0.65/0.66/0.43/0.55; ΔR² E over G+S BR +0.010, CO +0.010; PE −0.071 and TW +0.070 with CIs including zero -->

At one and four weeks ahead, recent cases carry nearly all the forecasting skill. The design detected known covariates only in Brazil and partly in Colombia (Section 3.1), so the null results in Peru and Taiwan say little. <!-- src: R3_A1_positive_control.csv BR_S1 L1->L2 0.57, L2->L3 0.52; CO_S1 L2->L3 0.30 -->

### 4.2 Why the embeddings can make forecasts worse

Annual embeddings do not change within a season, so the model probably uses them mainly to move its forecasts up or down. They raised the upper end of forecasts on zero weeks everywhere, but improved case-week coverage only with Peru held out (Section 3.5). <!-- src: S6_q90_mechanism.csv dq90_log > 0 in 14/14; R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv zero-week width 14/14 (significant in 13), case-week coverage PE_S5 +2.1 pp (week-weighted, Table S48) --> Such a shift loses on zero weeks and gains on case weeks whatever its overall merit [@lerch2017dilemma]. The Colombian loss persisted in district-years with reported cases, so it is not only an artefact of filled zeros. <!-- src: R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv CO_S1, CO_S2, CO_S4 case district-years p < 0.01 (max 0.0084) -->

Part of the loss seems to come simply from adding many columns: shuffled embeddings of the same size cost one-third to four-fifths as much as the real ones, and compact summaries shrank the loss (Sections 3.6 and 3.9). <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv C3 G_id_pct / G_emb_pct: colombia 0.34, peru 0.81; R3_C_tw2021_panel.csv taiwan 0.58/1.06 = 0.55; R3_C_variants.csv V1, V2 --> Sixty-four columns that barely change within a district probably give the trees many ways to separate districts, whatever the columns contain. A static embedding did not remove the loss, so year-to-year noise is not the whole explanation. AlphaEarth was trained partly to reconstruct climate reanalysis and elevation [@brown2025alphaearth], which may explain why they repeat information already in the climate and remote-sensing inputs. <!-- src: R3_C_variants.csv V3, V4; Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 (ERA5-Land and GLO-30 DEM as training targets), read 2026-11-18 -->

Tree size also matters. In Brazil, tuning each model separately changed WIS by about eight times the embedding gain, and in Peru it reversed the sign of the future-years estimate (Section 3.4), so comparisons that tune each input set separately partly measure the tuning. <!-- src: S1 section C (T2_ladder_long.csv: 0.00327/0.00043 = 7.7 pooled h{1,4}); R3_A5_planned_vs_reported.csv PE_S1 per-level +0.65 --> Tuning does not explain the Colombian loss (Section 3.9). <!-- src: R3_C_tuning.csv -->

Taiwan held out is a stress test: 98% of its rows were zero-filled, and its embeddings lay far from those of the training districts. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv TW_S5 zero_filled row_share 0.98; S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 cos_max_mean 0.70–0.82; S6_q90_mechanism.csv TW_S5 dq90_log 0.0929 --> Every township lost, whatever its similarity to the training districts [@meyer2021aoa], mostly in district-years without cases. <!-- src: S7b_embedding_distance_bins.csv TW_S5 G_excess_share_units_neg = 1; R3_A6_outcome_agnostic.csv TW_S5 −3.28 on case district-years vs −18.3 --> One possible explanation is that embeddings differ systematically between countries, so a model can use them to absorb each country's case definition and reporting level. Taiwan counts only locally acquired confirmed cases, so a level learned from the other countries would be wrong for every township.

### 4.3 Forecasts without case history

Without case history, in Brazil's future years, coordinates and a district label both reduced error, and the embeddings added nothing detectable beyond either. In Brazil's held-out states, where a label cannot help, nothing was detected, but the intervals were wide. <!-- src: T6b_nohist_estimands.csv BR_S1_nohist pooled G_id 3.30, G_excess −0.43; docs/results/review/RH_nohist_c6.md (C6 G_id +1.98; G_excess with C6 −0.04); BR_S2_nohist pooled G_id +0.88 [−0.48, 2.23], G_emb +1.52 [−1.27, 4.32], G_excess −0.01 [−1.28, 1.27], mde_pct 1.80 --> The future-years pattern is consistent with coordinates and embeddings acting partly as a location label, but the criterion we specified in advance was met in neither split. <!-- src: docs/methods/nohist_arm.md "Interpretation"; FINAL_RESULTS_BRIEF.md section 4 --> In Colombia's held-out states, coordinates, average past cases and the embeddings all made forecasts worse. <!-- src: R3_C_co_s2_nohist.csv --> Coordinates also follow climate zones, which makes identity hard to separate from environment. These analyses are exploratory, with one seed and settings tuned with case history.

### 4.4 Comparison with other studies

Our results do not contradict the PDFM studies, which used another embedding, monthly targets and a residual design [@brittain2026foundation; @muslim2026planetary], but small gains under temporal hold-outs need a location-only control. Like Pradhan [@pradhan2026benchmarks], we found no embedding advantage beyond location; in Brazil, the point estimate fell from +0.29% without a control to +0.02% with coordinates. <!-- src: S2_control_panel.csv brazil G_emb_pct 0.29 (seed 0), C2 G_excess_pct 0.02 --> Our results also agree with evidence that stand-ins for location help more within the area a model was trained on than beyond it [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies], that how far embeddings stand in for location depends on the outcome [@metzler2026satellite], and that generic image features predict many cross-sectional outcomes [@rolf2021mosaiks]. AlphaEarth gains for annual malaria and survey outcomes [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria] come from settings without weekly case histories.

### 4.5 Strengths and limitations

The study's strengths are four countries, fixed scored forecasts and model settings, several location controls, a positive control, equivalence tests and a dated log of protocol changes. We could not test PDFM, the embedding used in both earlier dengue studies, because it requires approved access [@agarwal2024pdfm]; our results apply to AlphaEarth only. Other learners and learned location encoders [@vivanco2023geoclip; @klemmer2025satclip] may behave differently.

Final, revised counts are not available in real time, so the forecasts look more accurate than they would in practice. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 35 (planned longer training window not run; Methods 2.12) --> <!-- src: R3_A3_trend.csv (smallest scaled p 0.096) --> AlphaEarth was trained on imagery that overlaps our test years [@brown2025alphaearth], which, if anything, favours the embeddings. <!-- src: R8_literature.md section 4 (Brown et al. §S15, §S22.2); R10_fact_check.md row 2 --> Espírito Santo's false zeros remain in the training targets. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 34 -->

Held-out districts were only partly unseen (Section 2.7). Had tree size been tuned within each fold, smaller trees would have been chosen in 12 of 15 Brazilian folds. <!-- src: RF_summary.md --> Removing all case history does not mimic sparse or delayed surveillance.

When the difference between models drifted across seasons, the main test gave too many false positives (14% to 18% at a nominal 5% in five comparisons), but in the loss comparisons we checked it was close to nominal (1% to 9%). <!-- src: R3_A8_iaaft_equivalence.csv size_nominal5, method ewc_hln: BR_S1 18.4, BR_S2 14.0, BR_S3 15.2, BR_S4 14.9, CO_S5 15.8; BR_S5 6.5, CO_S1 8.7, CO_S2 1.1, CO_S4 7.35 --> Our conclusions do not extend to other seasons, and with Peru or Taiwan held out only the excess gain has three seeds. <!-- src: R3_C_summary.md section 3 --> Some decisions, including the ±1% margin, one set of settings per country and Taiwan's 2021, followed results (Section 2.12). Further limitations are in Supplementary Methods, section I.

### 4.6 Implications and future work

We cannot rule out small gains. Upper 95% bounds reach +0.79% in Brazil's future years, +0.71% in its held-out districts and +2.02% with Peru held out. <!-- src: T1_primary_family.csv BR_S1, BR_S4, PE_S5 hi95_pct --> We make no recommendation about operational use. Follow-up work will test PDFM in Brazil, other model designs (a second model class, a residual design on zero-shot TimesFM, embeddings centred within each country) and more realistic settings (longer horizons, monthly targets, reporting delays).

### 4.7 Conclusions

Across four countries, AlphaEarth embeddings carried information about where dengue burden is high; in Brazil and Colombia, it largely duplicated coordinates and standard covariates. With case history, they added no detectable accuracy to weekly district dengue forecasts, so there was no gain whose source needed explaining. Effects larger than ±1%, about Brazil's combined gain from all non-case inputs, were ruled out in 8 of 14 comparisons, or 5 under the strict-variance check. <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin 1.0 within_Holm (8/14); R3_A4_equivalence.csv B4 Holm 5/14 --> Embeddings may worsen forecasts for a country whose surveillance differs from that of the training countries. Without case history, the results fit the idea that embeddings can act as a location label but do not prove it. Before a gain from foundation-model features is credited to place, it should be compared with location-only controls, using the same model settings throughout, in places left out of training and against a margin stated in advance.
