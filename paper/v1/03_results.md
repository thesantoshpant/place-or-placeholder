### 3.3 Equivalence and secondary gains

After Holm adjustment of the equivalence tests, 8 of the 13 cells lay within ±1% of the reference WIS (Table 2; Table S24). Colombia with held-out regions lay within ±1% only without adjustment (90% CI lower bound −0.97%). No Peruvian or Taiwanese cell was shown to be within ±1%. <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv margin_pct 1.0 within_unadjusted, within_Holm, lo90_pct --> For Brazil (new years and held-out states) and Colombia held out, equivalence assumes that the weekly loss difference does not drift across seasons. <!-- src: docs/results/review/RC_summary.md verdict; NOTES_FOR_WRITERS.md item 30 --> <!-- src: T1b_margin_sensitivity.csv within_unadjusted, within_Holm at margin_pct 0.5 and 2.0 (at 2.0, PE_S5 within unadjusted only) -->

The secondary gains are unadjusted (Table S21). The embedding gain was never significantly positive, and its 95% CI lay below zero in seven cells: the four Colombian cells within the country, Peru and Taiwan in the new-years split, and Taiwan held out. <!-- src: T1 G_emb_pct, G_emb_lo95_pct, G_emb_hi95_pct --> The identity gain was never significantly positive either (largest +0.10%, Brazil held out). Its CI lay below zero in six cells: Colombia's held-out states, regions and districts, and the three held-out-country cells outside Brazil. In these cells the selected control made the reference model worse. <!-- src: T1 G_id_pct, G_id_lo95_pct, G_id_hi95_pct (CO_S2, CO_S3, CO_S4, CO_S5, PE_S5, TW_S5) -->

The excess gain can then look favourable for the wrong reason: the embeddings partly undo the control's damage. With Colombia held out, coordinates worsened forecasts by 0.81% (95% CI 0.46 to 1.16), and the excess gain was +0.22% although the embeddings alone changed error by −0.04%. <!-- src: T1 CO_S5 pct_of_WIS_ref, G_emb_pct, G_id_pct, G_id_lo95_pct, G_id_hi95_pct --> The identity share was therefore not estimable. <!-- src: T3_estimands.csv estimand ID_share, reason -->

### 3.5 Where the losses arise

Figure 3 splits each excess gain into additive contributions from the three types of target week (Tables S28 and S29). Zero-filled weeks contributed negatively in all 13 cells, with a 95% CI below zero in 11. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv G_excess zero_filled contrib_pct, contrib_lo95_pct, contrib_hi95_pct (CI includes 0 for CO_S5, TW_S1) --> Weeks with cases contributed positively in 12 of 13 cells. With Brazil held out, zeros listed by the source also lost (−2.69% of that stratum's WIS, 95% CI −5.35 to −0.03; unadjusted p = 0.048). But all such zeros fall in 2020–2021, and 2021, a COVID-19 pandemic year [@chen2022covid; @roster2024covidbrazil], had the lowest excess gain in every Brazilian cell (Table S30; Figure S2), so zero type and year cannot be separated. <!-- src: T5_strata.csv G_excess recorded_zero contrib_pct (BR cells, −0.22 to +0.05); BR_S5 pct_of_ref, lo95_pct, hi95_pct, p; S8_per_year.csv G_excess BR cells (2021 lowest in BR_S1, S2, S3, S5) -->

### 3.7 Taiwan and transfer between countries

Including 2021 made both Taiwan estimates more negative. Without 2021 they were −0.44% (95% CI −1.01 to 0.14) for new years and −11.16% (−15.66 to −6.65) with Taiwan held out (Table S37). <!-- src: T6a_sensitivities.csv TW_S1_no2021, TW_S5_no2021 G_excess; T1 TW_S1, TW_S5 --> In absolute terms, the held-out loss went from −0.0027 to −0.0035, while the reference WIS fell from 0.0242 to 0.0202. ### 3.8 Forecasts without case history

Without case history (Brazil, new years), the reference model's WIS rose from 0.1681 to 0.3526 on the same rows (Figure 4; Table S41). <!-- src: T2_ladder.csv BR_S1 L3; T6c_nohist_ladder.csv N3 pooled --> The selected control, coordinates, <!-- src: T4_cstar_selection.csv BR_S1_nohist --> improved the reference model by 3.30% (95% CI 1.68 to 4.93; p < 0.001). The embeddings without a control improved it by 2.54% (−0.82 to 5.89; p = 0.13). With coordinates in the model, adding the embeddings changed WIS by −0.43% (−2.06 to 1.19; p = 0.58), and the minimum detectable effect was 2.29% (Table S42).
