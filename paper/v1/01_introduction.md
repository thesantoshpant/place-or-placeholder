## 1 Introduction

Dengue causes a large burden of illness in the tropics and subtropics. [[GBD: authors to pull from IHME]] [@gbd2023diseases; @ihme2023gbdresults] Recent seasons in our study countries were exceptional: Peru had the largest dengue outbreak on its record in 2023 [@munayco2024peru], and Brazil its greatest epidemic to date in 2024 [@gurgelgoncalves2024brazil]. Short-term forecasts for small areas can help time vector control and prepare clinical services, and probabilistic dengue forecasting is well established [@johansson2019challenge; @lowe2016elife; @colongonzalez2021vietnam; @araujo2026sprint].

Where dengue spreads depends partly on environment and on how people move [@harish2024movement], so forecasters often add descriptors of place, each needing its own data product. Geospatial foundation models offer a shortcut: they compress large data archives into a fixed-length vector, an embedding, for any location. AlphaEarth Foundations provides a 64-dimensional embedding for every 10 m pixel and every year from 2017, learned from satellite imagery [@brown2025alphaearth; @aef_dataset]. <!-- src: docs/research/literature.md verification round 2 item 4 (V1 ANNUAL, unit-length, 2017 onward); docs/research/data_and_tools.md AlphaEarth row (bands A00–A63, 10 m) --> The Population Dynamics Foundation Model (PDFM) provides place embeddings built from search, maps and activity data [@agarwal2024pdfm]. Such vectors exist everywhere, which makes them attractive where data are thin.

An embedding is also a fingerprint. A district's vector changes slowly across years, so a flexible model can use it to recognise the district (for example, to learn that it has high baseline incidence) without using what the vector says about the environment. When every test district also appears in training, as in a split by time, this identifier role alone can improve scores. We call this the identity confound. Related problems are known in spatial machine learning: spatial proxies such as coordinates raise apparent skill but extrapolate poorly [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies], random cross-validation overstates skill for spatial data [@roberts2017cv; @ploton2020spatial], and in England much of the gain from satellite-image embeddings for house prices, though not for air pollution, reflected coarse location [@metzler2026satellite]. The identity confound can be tested with features that carry only location, with places never seen in training, and by lagging the embedding so that forecasts use only earlier imagery. For dengue there is a further complication: once a model sees each district's recent case counts, the case history already identifies the district. "Environmental information or identifier?" can then be answered cleanly only in models without case history.

Published dengue evaluations of place embeddings have not yet applied these tests. Brittain et al. added PDFM embeddings as a residual adjustment to five model families for monthly dengue in 5,570 Brazilian municipalities and reported lower weighted interval scores in 19 of 20 model-horizon combinations; they used one October 2023 snapshot throughout a 2020–2025 backtest with temporal hold-outs only, and we found no location-only control in their text [@brittain2026foundation]. <!-- src: docs/research/literature.md verification round 2 item 1 --> The Mexico dengue case study of Muslim et al. used a static October 2023 snapshot, with an October 2025 sensitivity check; its gain (a mean reduction in weighted interval score of 0.0051 at one month) was not significant at three or six months. It had no location-only control or held-out region, although other case studies in that paper used shuffled- or random-embedding controls and held-out jurisdictions [@muslim2026planetary]. <!-- src: docs/research/literature.md verification round 2 item 2 --> Nazir et al. reported AlphaEarth gains for climate-sensitive health outcomes, with static embeddings for malaria and survey-year embeddings for respiratory infection, and neither analysis had a coordinate, identifier or shuffled-embedding control or a spatial hold-out [@nazir2026alphaearth]; a related workshop study reported better annual malaria forecasts under a one-year forward hold-out [@nazir2026malaria]. The closest precedent is a single-city study in India, where a satellite-embedding advantage over open map layers "fell to 0.001 once the open layers were given the cell's own coordinates" [@pradhan2026benchmarks]. The 2024 Dengue Forecasting Sprint in Brazil did not test embeddings and scored forecasts with the log score, the continuous ranked probability score and the interval score [@araujo2026sprint]. Our study is not a replication of the PDFM work: we use satellite-only embeddings lagged by one year, weekly targets and a gradient-boosted forecaster.

We evaluate, retrospectively, whether AlphaEarth embeddings add skill to weekly forecasts beyond surveillance history and location in 6,987 second-level administrative units of Brazil, Colombia, Peru and Taiwan <!-- src: docs/data/boundaries_join.md (6,987 units with geometry) --> (municipalities in Brazil and Colombia, provinces in Peru, townships in Taiwan; we call all of them districts). Our contributions are:

1. **A controlled comparison.** A ladder of quantile gradient-boosting models adds case history, climate, remote sensing and population, then the previous year's embeddings, with the same rows and one hyperparameter set per country at every rung.
2. **Location-only controls and an excess-gain estimand.** Controls giving the model a district identifier, its position or its pre-study dengue level are added with and without embeddings. The excess gain, what embeddings add once such a control is present, is tested for difference and for equivalence within ±1%.
3. **Splits that remove the identifier route step by step:** new years, then held-out groups of states, buffered spatial blocks, random districts and whole countries. The 14 country-by-split cells form one pre-specified family for multiplicity correction.
4. **An exploratory arm without case history,** where an identifier and genuine place information can be told apart.

We ask four questions. **RQ1:** when only the years are new, do embeddings reduce forecast error beyond the non-embedding features? **RQ2:** do location-only controls add skill, and is any embedding gain left beyond them? **RQ3:** does any gain appear in districts, states or countries never seen in training? **RQ4 (exploratory):** in which weeks and districts do embeddings help or hurt? A comparison of year-lagged with static embeddings is deferred. The share of an embedding gain that a control reproduces needs a clear gain to divide; with case history there is none, so it is examined only in the arm without case history.
