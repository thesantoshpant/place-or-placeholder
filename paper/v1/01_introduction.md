## 1 Introduction

Dengue causes a large burden of illness in the tropics and subtropics. The Global Burden of Disease study estimated [to be completed] dengue cases and [to be completed] deaths worldwide in [to be completed] [@gbd2023diseases; @ihme2023gbdresults]. <!-- AUTHORS: GBD numbers (cases, deaths and reference year) to be pulled from the IHME GBD Results tool; no figure has been verified --> Recent seasons in our study countries were exceptional: Peru had the largest dengue outbreak on its record in 2023 [@munayco2024peru], and Brazil its largest epidemic to date in 2024 [@gurgelgoncalves2024brazil]. Short-term forecasts for small areas can help health services time vector control and prepare clinical care. Probabilistic dengue forecasting is well established [@johansson2019challenge; @lowe2016elife; @colongonzalez2021vietnam; @araujo2026sprint].

Dengue transmission depends partly on the environment and on human movement [@harish2024movement]. Forecasters therefore often add descriptors of place, each from its own data product. Geospatial foundation models offer a shortcut. They compress large archives of observations into a fixed-length list of numbers, called an embedding, for any location. AlphaEarth Foundations provides a 64-number embedding for every 10 m pixel and every year from 2017 [@brown2025alphaearth; @aef_dataset]. It is computed from optical and radar satellite imagery by a model that was also trained to reconstruct climate, elevation and other layers. <!-- src: docs/research/literature.md verification round 2 item 4 (V1 ANNUAL, unit-length, 2017 onward); docs/research/data_and_tools.md AlphaEarth row (bands A00–A63, 10 m); inputs vs training targets: Brown et al. arXiv:2507.22291v2 Table S1 and section S15 (inputs Sentinel-2, Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-1; ERA5-Land, GLO-30 DEM, GEDI, GRACE, PALSAR-2, NLCD, text used as training targets only), read 2026-11-18; include in the human reference check --> The Population Dynamics Foundation Model (PDFM) provides place embeddings built from search, map and activity data [@agarwal2024pdfm]. Because they exist everywhere, such embeddings are attractive where local data are thin.

An embedding is also a fingerprint. A district's embedding changes little from year to year, so a flexible model can use it to recognise the district and learn its usual dengue level, without using what the embedding says about the environment. When every test district also appears in training, as when only later years are held out, this identifier role alone can improve scores. We call this the identity confound. In spatial machine learning, coordinates and similar proxies raise apparent skill but extrapolate poorly [@meyer2019spatial; @mila2024proxies]. Random cross-validation overstates skill for spatial data [@roberts2017cv; @ploton2020spatial]. In England, much of the gain from satellite-image embeddings for house prices, though not for air pollution, reflected coarse location [@metzler2026satellite].

The identity confound can be tested with features that carry only location and with places never seen in training. Separately, lagging the embedding by one year keeps forecasts from using imagery of the year being forecast. Dengue adds a complication: once a model sees each district's recent case counts, the case history already identifies the district. Whether embeddings act as environmental information or as an identifier can then be answered cleanly only in models without case history.

Published dengue evaluations of place embeddings have not yet applied these tests. Brittain et al. added PDFM embeddings to monthly forecasts for 5,570 Brazilian municipalities as a residual adjustment, using one October 2023 snapshot and temporal hold-outs only [@brittain2026foundation]. Weighted interval scores fell in 19 of 20 model-horizon combinations, and we found no location-only control in their report. <!-- src: docs/research/literature.md verification round 2 item 1 --> In the Mexico dengue case study of Muslim et al., a static embedding reduced the mean weighted interval score by 0.0051 at one month, and the gain was not significant at three or six months [@muslim2026planetary]. That case study had no location-only control or held-out region, although other case studies in the paper did. <!-- src: docs/research/literature.md verification round 2 item 2 --> Nazir et al. reported AlphaEarth gains for malaria and respiratory infection without a coordinate, identifier or shuffled-embedding control and without a spatial hold-out [@nazir2026alphaearth; @nazir2026malaria]. The closest precedent is a single-city study in India, where a satellite-embedding advantage over open map layers "fell to 0.001 once the open layers were given the cell's own coordinates" [@pradhan2026benchmarks].

We evaluated, retrospectively, whether AlphaEarth embeddings add skill to weekly dengue forecasts beyond surveillance history and location in 6,987 second-level administrative units of Brazil, Colombia, Peru and Taiwan (6,909 scored in the test years). <!-- src: docs/data/boundaries_join.md (6,987 units with geometry); S7_embedding_distance.csv n_units BR_S5 5,439 + CO_S5 1,070 + PE_S5 115 + TW_S5 285 = 6,909 after the Espírito Santo exclusion --> These are municipalities in Brazil and Colombia, provinces in Peru and townships in Taiwan; we call them all districts. Models added one block of inputs at a time, ending with the previous year's embeddings, with model type, scored rows and configuration fixed within each country. Location controls gave the model a district identifier, its position or its pre-study dengue level, with and without the embeddings. Our main measure, the excess gain, is what the embeddings add once such a control is present; we tested it for a difference and for equivalence within ±1%. Evaluation splits removed the identifier route step by step: new years, held-out states, held-out regions, held-out districts and a held-out country. The 14 combinations of country and split form one family for multiple-testing correction, fixed before any held-out-place result was seen. <!-- src: docs/plans/deviations_from_protocol.md row 12 (fcc3c38, before any spatial result) --> An exploratory analysis without case history examined whether identifier and place information can be told apart.

We asked four questions. First, when only the years are new, do embeddings reduce forecast error beyond the other inputs? Second, do location controls add skill, and is any embedding gain left beyond them? Third, does any gain appear in districts, states or countries never seen in training? Fourth, as an exploratory question, in which weeks and districts do embeddings help or hurt?
